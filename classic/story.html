Made tobe kept.

Sovereign was started for the days a person walks into a room and needs to be taken at their word. A first interview. A closing argument. A promotion, a signing, a verdict.

We make one kind of thing: formal tailoring for women and men, cut to the person who will wear it, and finished by hand so it is still right in ten years. Not fast. Not loud. Sovereign.

Chosen for the second wear.

Our worsted wools come from mills in northern Italy and the north of England, in weights that behave in a boardroom in May and a courtroom in December.

Every cloth in the first collection is offered in six colours, from charcoal to ivory. The copper brown is ours alone.

A chest that moves with you.

A half-canvas of horsehair and wool is sewn into the front of every blazer by hand. It lets the lapel roll instead of fold, and the chest shape itself to you over the first months.

Sleeve heads are set by hand. Buttonholes are cut and sewn after the garment is finished. Cuff buttons work.

One stitch. Only you know it is there.

The lioness in our mark wears a single cross stitch on her ear. So does every garment we make: one copper stitch, placed inside, by hand, as the last thing that happens before it leaves the studio. It is a signature you do not have to show anyone.
